# Proposal: FFRProof — the federal financial report pre-flight audit

**Date Discovered:** 2026-11-02
**Verticals (this sweep):** `VERTICAL_A` = `public_sector_grants` (first scan — produced this candidate) · `VERTICAL_B` = `sales_order_service_level_otif` (first scan — **no build**, see §6)
**Signal Vector:** B (document/record pain: a form assembled from three systems that disagree) + C (billable-quantity decision: federal share, match, unliquidated obligations)
**Signal Intensity Score:** **74 / 100** — repeat intent 75 / incumbent pricing friction 70 / willingness-to-pay 70 / urgency 80
**Target Persona:** The grants/finance manager at a small-to-mid federal award recipient — a nonprofit, a community health center, a school district or a small municipality holding 3–25 active federal awards, with no grants-accounting platform and one person assembling the SF-425 from the general ledger, the payment-system drawdown statement and the award terms.

---

## 1. The Bottleneck & Market Context

- **Current state.** The SF-425 (Federal Financial Report) is filed every quarter (or semi-annually/annually, per the award), **within 30 days of the end of the reporting period** (EDA FFR instructions). Every number on it is derived from three sources that are maintained by different people: the general ledger (expenditures by cost category), the federal payment system's drawdown history (cash receipts, lines 10a–10c), and the award terms (total authorized, required recipient share). The form then has to cross-foot: cash on hand = receipts − disbursements (10c = 10a − 10b), total federal share = expenditures + unliquidated obligations (10g = 10e + 10f), unobligated balance = authorized − federal share (10h = 10d − 10g), remaining match = required − provided (10k = 10i − 10j).
- **What actually goes wrong (measured, in-window).** The error taxonomy is stable and named by practitioners, not vendors: reporting the **drawdown amount as the expenditure figure** "without performing the reconciliation necessary to confirm it" (Cherry Bekaert, in-window); **not reconciling the payment-system drawdowns to the report** (Witts CPA, 2026-07-16); reporting the period instead of the cumulative total, **counting the same dollar twice**, and **overstating match** (business-software.com, 2026-10-19). The consequence is not a formatting note: a delinquent FFR makes the payment system treat the award as delinquent and **blocks drawdowns** (DOJ OJP financial guide), and a pattern of late financial reporting is a documented factor in a **high-risk designation** under 2 CFR 200.208, which brings more frequent reporting and can affect future awards (CASRAI, checked 2026-07-17). At the audit end, a 2026 HHS-OIG review of one grantee found **$696,017 in unallowable costs and nearly $5 million unsupported**, with a recommended **$967,387 refund** (CharityTracker, 2026-09-07) — the same reconciliation gap, one year later.
- **Validation intensity.** Five independent in-window sources describe the same reconciliation gap from different seats (two CPA firms, a grants-software comparison, a compliance advisory, and the inspector-general finding). Repeat intent is scored 75, not 100: three of the five sell accounting or software services, so their framing is discounted.
- **Why now (dated, not evergreen).** (a) The **quarterly clock** is continuous — the federal fiscal year closed 2026-09-30, so Q4 reports were due 2026-10-30 and the Q1 FY2027 report is due **2027-01-30**; a recipient that missed October is in the delinquent-drawdown state right now. (b) The **Uniform Guidance rewrite** (proposed 2026-05-29) carries an implementation date currently pointed at **2026-12-11** after the August continuing resolution, adds SAM.gov subaward reporting and tighter downstream classification (Rehmann; Crowell & Moring; GFOA), and every recipient is re-reading its award terms this quarter — which is exactly when award-term extraction is worth doing. The final rule is **not yet issued**: the product must therefore read the award's own terms, never a rule table it maintains (rule 5).
- **Why the shape is not another invoice-reconciliation product.** The buyer is a grant recipient, the input is a government form with published line arithmetic, and the finding is "this line does not cross-foot to that source, here is the page" — not a variance narrative over vendor invoices. The adjacent factory products (QuarterLine, LedgerLink, FacturGate, SpendProof) serve tax, ledger and vendor-invoice buyers; none touches an award's terms or the federal drawdown record.

## 2. Architecture & Technical Blueprint

- **Engine type:** document-parse extraction **in front of** a deterministic form-arithmetic engine (`context/tech_stack_capabilities.md` §2). The model reads fields out of PDFs; **every number the product reports is computed in TypeScript from parsed or user-supplied values.** A field the parser cannot read is `unstated` and blocks the check that depends on it — never a guess, never a zero.
- **Deterministic core (`src/lib/calc/ffrproof/`):**
  - `crossFoot(report)` — the four line identities above, each with a zero tolerance and the form line it comes from; a break returns both sides of the equation and the difference, not "invalid".
  - `reconcileCash(report, drawdownStatement)` — line 10a/10b/10c against the payment system's per-subaccount receipts and disbursements; reports the drift by subaccount and the cents.
  - `matchTest(report, awardTerms)` — required recipient share (10i) against provided (10j) and remaining (10k); a final report with 10k > 0 is a hard finding, and 10f/10h must be zero on a final.
  - `budgetVariance(glExport, approvedBudget)` — actual vs approved per cost category, over/under, and the percentage at which the **award's own terms** require prior approval; if the terms do not state it, the check returns `unstated` rather than applying a default.
  - `periodVsCumulative(priorReport, currentReport)` — catches the "reported the period, not the total" and "counted the same dollar twice" errors by differencing consecutive filings.
  - Fixed-point money arithmetic throughout; no float in a reported dollar figure; invariant `Σ(category variances) == total variance` pinned by a test vector.
- **Extraction (LLM/parse):** award terms / notice of award PDF → authorized amount, period of performance, required match percentage, reporting frequency, prior-approval threshold, agency-specific line instructions; payment-system statement PDF → subaccount receipts/disbursements. Each field carries provenance (page + verbatim quote). The GL export is parsed deterministically (CSV), not by a model.
- **Inputs → outputs:** prior FFR (optional) + current worksheet or GL export + drawdown statement + award terms → a line-by-line pre-flight report: which lines cross-foot, which do not and to what source, the corrected values, the match position, and a per-finding citation. Nothing is filed on the recipient's behalf; the output is the audited worksheet the human signs.
- **Interfaces:** `src/app/ffrproof/` (Editorial Signature UI: upload → award pick → finding set), `POST /api/ffrproof/audit`, WebMCP tool `audit_federal_financial_report`.

## 3. Dual-Pronged Monetization

- **Web tier (Stripe):** **$19** one-off audit for a single award-quarter (report export included) · **$49/mo** for a rolling portfolio of up to 5 active awards, re-audited each period with the filing clock on every award.
- **Agentic tier (WebMCP / metered API):** **$1.50/call.**
- **Tool class: LLM / document-parse-backed** → band **$0.50–$3.00/call**, and only if the rate clears **3× the measured per-call cost** (`context/company_goals.md`; `context/tech_stack_capabilities.md` §3).
  **Modelled per-call cost ≈ $0.17** — award-terms PDF 2–4 pages ≈ $0.012; drawdown statement 1–3 pages ≈ $0.009; declared-field extraction ~7k in / 800 out ≈ $0.02–0.03 per document set; ×1.5 retry factor; schedule-free (no maintained rule table to re-parse). **$1.50 clears 3× with ~8.8× headroom.** The first live award package is the measurement run: re-cost before the tool is registered, and if measured cost exceeds $0.50 the rate is wrong, not the class.
  `usesLlmPrimitive: true` at build time; `src/products/pricing.test.ts` holds the band.

## 4. Free-incumbent check (the rule that decides these)

- **Free, and it owns a different job:** the federal portals own **submission** — Grants.gov Workspace validates *application* forms pre-award and stops there ("if your grants work starts after the award letter, you will need other systems for reporting, accounting and internal tracking" — AlignMint, 2026). The payment system **pre-populates** lines 10a–10c of the FFR for several agencies, which removes one data-entry step and none of the reconciliation. Agencies publish instruction PDFs and free Excel budget-vs-actual templates (GrantCue, Vena) — templates, not an audit.
- **Paid, and it is priced out of this buyer's reach:** grant-management suites are quoted annually and sold as a platform (entry $2,000–$10,000/yr; mid-market $10,000–$40,000/yr; Sage Intacct for nonprofits $15,000–$40,000+ — ClickTime 2026 comparison; Euna Grants is positioned at "larger nonprofits and government-adjacent organizations" — LiveImpact 2026; Avila targets local governments). A 3-award nonprofit on a spreadsheet does not buy a $15k suite to check four equations, and the human alternative (an outsourced grant accountant) is a rule-1 service, not a product.
- **The unowned slice:** the **pre-flight arithmetic over the recipient's own three documents**, per award, for the price of one hour of a bookkeeper, with the form line and the source page attached to every finding.
- **Rule-5 check (this is the one that could kill it):** there is no maintained rule table. The form's arithmetic is fixed and published; every agency-specific field, threshold and match percentage is **read from the award's own terms**, and an award term the parser cannot read returns `unstated`, which blocks that finding instead of defaulting. A stale Uniform Guidance can therefore never produce a wrong number — at worst it produces "the award terms do not state this".
- **Rule-1 check:** the product stops at a verifiable finding set and a corrected worksheet. It does not file, does not certify, and does not argue with a program officer.

## 6. VERTICAL_B — `sales_order_service_level_otif`: scanned, no build

- **The pain is real and priced.** Walmart's OTIF program fines **3% of the cost of goods** on every case that misses the on-time or in-full threshold (widely reported 2026 targets: 90% on-time prepaid, 98% collect-ready, 95% in-full), the fine is an AR invoice routed through the compliance billing portal rather than the AP dispute portal, and the **dispute window is roughly 15–30 days** from posting (Refunds Manager 2026-10-12; Glimpse 2026-09; iNymbus 2026-09-30). Measured exposure: a $1M Walmart program running at 92% OTIF is roughly **$18,000–$24,000/yr** in OTIF chargebacks alone (ShipCalm 2026). Only **20–30% of deductions are ever disputed, and about 40% of those are won** (SPS Commerce, 2026, via Chargeflow) — so the audit shape has real money behind it.
- **Why it still does not get a build slot: the exact proposed shape is already free, and the paid layer is cheaper per claim than a new tool would be.**
  - *Free, and it is the candidate*: Warp's **Retailer Chargeback Audit** takes a deduction CSV/PDF, checks the fine amount against the stated percent, the reason code against MABD/arrival/quantity, duplicates across rows, and the deduction date against the dispute window, sorts every row into *dispute / review / note*, and **copies the dispute letter** — published in-window by a freight broker using it as a lead magnet for its own carrier service.
  - *Paid*: SupplyPike (now SPS Commerce) automates chargeback identification, dispute filing and **proactive fine prevention**; iNymbus prices at **$0.40–$0.70 per claim** and offers a **free deductions audit** before you commit; HighRadius, Smyyth and RetailPath cover the enterprise end; ClearChain and Vendormint are flat-fee managed services.
  - The one slice the free tool leaves — a **forward-looking exposure forecast** (will this month's open POs fall under the threshold, and which cases are at risk) — is what SupplyPike's proactive alerts already sell, and its input is the retailer's own scorecard, which the retailer gives the supplier free. Scored **71** (repeat 85 / friction 55 / WTP 75 / urgency 70) and dropped at the **free-incumbent rule** plus an owned paid layer, not at the score.
- **Re-open trigger (dated, not vague):** a retailer publishes a **machine-readable compliance/scorecard export** that a third party can consume without portal access, or the free front door disappears (Warp withdraws the audit tool) while the paid platforms keep raising per-claim pricing. Absent that, this vertical is retired cheaply, which is a result.
- **Runner-up recorded, not proposed:** chargeback **exposure forecasting** from the open-PO book (71). Same blocker: the appointment/MABD record lives in the retailer's portal, so the tool would be hand-fed data the portal already displays.
