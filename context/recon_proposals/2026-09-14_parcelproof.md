### 1. The Bottleneck & Market Context
- **Current State:** Carrier invoices are billed on **billable weight** — the greater of actual weight and dimensional weight (L×W×H ÷ divisor) — with a divisor and rounding rule set by contract, then stacked with accessorials (AHS-Dimension, oversize, fuel, residential). A single wrong input flips the billing basis: a package measured at 12″ instead of 11″ rounds up under the 2026 rules, and USPS's move from divisor 166 to 139 raised billable weight on every bulky-but-light parcel on 2026-07-12. The shipper's systems hold the *declared* dimensions and weights; the carrier's measurement infrastructure holds *billed* ones, and nothing reconciles the two. Published audit experience puts dim-weight calculation errors at 0.1–0.4% of lines, with total recovery at 2–8% of carrier spend — and the disputes expire on a ~21–30 day clock. Today's tools are either a contingency auditor (you pay a share of recovery, and only for high-volume accounts), an enterprise platform (annual contract), or a spreadsheet nobody maintains. Recording the wrong divisor in your own pre-shipment cost estimate is a second, self-inflicted failure: shippers were told to update 166 → 139 before July 12 or keep under-quoting outbound cost on every bulky order.
- **Validation Intensity:** High repeat intent across eight-plus independent 2026 sources, each naming the same audit steps (recompute DIM weight with the contract divisor, verify zone, validate accessorial eligibility, check service-commitment refunds) and the same blockers (manual effort, structured-data-but-no-tooling, expiring dispute windows). Parcel audit is called out as *the most automatable* audit precisely because UPS/FedEx invoice data is structured — which is the definition of a deterministic engine waiting to be built.

### 2. Architecture & Technical Blueprint
- **Engine Type:** Deterministic audit/calculator (archetype **a**, with a reconciliation transform from archetype **b**).
- **Deterministic Core (`src/lib/calc/parcelaudit/`):**
  - `types.ts` — `ShipmentRecord { orderId, tracking, carrier, service, shipDate, dims{l,w,h}, actualWeight, zone, declaredValue, accessorials[], promisedDate, deliveredAt }` and `InvoiceLine { tracking, billedWeight, billedDims?, zone, baseCharge, surcharges[{code, amount}], fuelPct?, total }`.
  - `dimWeight.ts` — `billableWeight = max(actual, ceil(L)×ceil(W)×ceil(H)/divisor)`; divisor resolved by **carrier × service × date** (UPS 139, FedEx 139, USPS 166 before 2026-07-12 → 139 from 2026-07-12), round-up rule applied, cubic-inch thresholds enforced (FedEx Ground / USPS: >1,728 cu in).
  - `surcharges.ts` — accessorial eligibility recomputed from the record: AHS-Dimension trigger (UPS longest side > 96″, FedEx > 48″), AHS-Weight, oversize, residential, address-correction flags; surcharges billed with no eligibility basis are flagged with the trigger that failed.
  - `serviceCommitment.ts` — late-delivery refund eligibility from `promisedDate` vs `deliveredAt` per service commitment, with the money-back window applied.
  - `disputeClock.ts` — per-line dispute-window status from invoice date (UPS ≈30 days, FedEx ≈21 days) → `expiring` / `expired` / `open`, so recovery effort goes where the clock still permits.
  - `rateCard.ts` — optional customer-supplied contract rate card (CSV): when present, base-charge and zone are recomputed; when absent the line is reported as **`unverifiable-rate`** — flagged, never guessed and never silently passed.
  - `ledger.ts` — recovery ledger per tracking number: recomputed vs billed, delta in cents, rule id, evidence fields; plus exposure summary (window total, annualised run-rate by carrier). Invariant: every flagged delta is reproducible from the inputs, and unverifiable lines are counted as unverifiable, not as zero.
- **Inputs / Outputs:** `POST { invoiceLines[]{…}, shipmentRecords[]{…}, carrier, rateCard? }` → `{ recoveredTotal, overchargedLines[], byRule[], unverifiableLines[], disputeWindows[], packet: { csv|pdf } }` — a per-carrier dispute file plus a one-page recovery summary.
- **Known-answer test vectors (`vitest`):** fixtures asserting exact recovery per rule — a divisor swap (166 → 139 accepted as billed) on a 1,800 cu in parcel, a round-up case (11.2″ billed as 12″), an AHS-Dimension billed on a 40″ parcel (ineligible at FedEx, eligible at UPS), a late-delivery refund inside vs outside the money-back window, and a clean invoice that must produce **zero** flags.

### 3. Dual-Pronged Monetization
- **Web Tier (Stripe):** free audit of the first 50 invoice lines with the recovery total (a real number, not a teaser) — the hook; **$9 one-off** full per-carrier dispute packet (CSV + branded PDF workpaper); **$49/mo** ongoing audit tier for 3PLs and consultants running several client accounts. Margin clears the $0.25/query floor at any plausible volume.
- **Agentic Tier (WebMCP):** `audit_carrier_invoice(lines, records)` at **$0.25/call** and `compute_billable_weight(dims, weight, carrier, date)` at **$0.05/call**, Stripe-metered. A procurement or logistics agent audits each week's invoice headless and files disputes; a rate-shopping agent uses the weight primitive to quote landed cost correctly for the post-2026-07-12 rules. Contingency auditors and enterprise platforms expose no metered deterministic endpoint.

### 4. Zero-Friction Viral Hook
- **Recovery Scorecard:** a shareable `?carrier=ups&window=2026-09` deep-link stating lines audited, overcharge rate, recovered amount and the expiring-dispute count — the artifact a fractional logistics consultant forwards to a prospect.
- **Embeddable widget:** "DIM overcharge exposure this month" `<iframe>` for 3PL client portals, with the branded attribution line every embed carries → backlink + signup.
- **pSEO surface:** one page per rule and carrier-year (`/parcelproof/calc/usps-dim-divisor-2026`, `/parcelproof/calc/ahs-dimension-trigger`, `/parcelproof/calc/fedex-21-day-dispute-window`) — high-intent, dated, evergreen-updatable, and a natural fit for the existing pSEO machinery.
