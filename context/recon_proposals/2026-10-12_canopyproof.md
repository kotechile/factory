### 1. The Bottleneck & Market Context

- **Current State:** the operator preparing for the **30 December 2026** application date has to file a due diligence statement that carries the geolocation of *every* plot of land behind the shipment. That data arrives from suppliers in whatever form they have: a CSV with half an address in the wrong columns, a spreadsheet with an area column that disagrees with the coordinates, KML from one grower and a national grid from another, a plotted polygon that is actually a cooperative's whole concession outline. The operator's job is not to file — it is to **audit a submission set it did not produce**, before the statement is filed, because the file upload either passes or it does not.
- **Named failure mechanics (all arithmetic and geometry, no judgement):** a plot above four hectares submitted as a single point (the statement can be rejected outright); two suppliers whose polygons overlap, so the same land is counted twice behind two batches; a supplier-declared area that contradicts its own boundary vertices; a declared tonnage that cannot physically come off the declared plot area; coordinates in the wrong order or a projection that is not WGS84; a plot whose stated country of production does not match where the coordinates land. All of these are checkable arithmetic — and none of them is checkable by eye across hundreds of plots. One EUDR platform states it plainly: *"polygons fail validation more than they pass… manual review per polygon doesn't scale to thousands of suppliers."*
- **Validation Intensity (screened for non-seller sources, per the `healthcare_rcm` lesson):** an EUDR data vendor's own practitioner write-up and an independent industry poll both name **"the gathering and verification of complete geolocation data" the single biggest issue companies face**; the Commission's own FAQ is the source of the rule that *a single polygon cannot cover several plots of land*; the EU Information System's user guide (updated 16 September 2026) is the source of the upload/validation/rejection mechanics. The dated rule movement is the Commission's own: **Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/2102** amended the product list in Annex I in **64 places**, in force **18 September 2026**, with some added codes becoming regulated only from **30 December 2027**.

### 2. Architecture & Technical Blueprint

- **Engine Type:** archetype **(e) LLM/document-parse extraction in front of a deterministic engine** (owner, 2026-10-05) — parser (b) + audit engine (a) + report emitter (d).
- **The model may extract; it may never produce a number the product reports.** The LLM/parse step reads the *supplier submission pack* (CSV/XLSX rows, PDFs, an email body) and emits a declared field set per plot: `{plot_id, commodity, country_of_production, declared_area_ha, geometry_kind (point|polygon), coordinates, production_period, legality_evidence_ref, dds_reference?, quantity, unit}`. Every field is labelled **`extracted`**; any field it is unsure of, and any unreadable region, is reported **`unreadable`/`unstated`** and **blocks the verdict** (rule 5 — never a default, never an estimate).
- **Deterministic core (`src/lib/calc/canopyproof/`, pure TypeScript, fixture-testable without network):**
  - **Geometry validity:** ring closed, ≥4 vertices, no self-intersection, coordinate order, decimal precision, WGS84 assumed and *stated* — a non-WGS84 declaration is `unsupported`, never reprojected silently.
  - **Area & the four-hectare rule:** plot area computed from the vertices (spherical excess); polygon required above 4 ha (non-cattle), single point permitted at or below 4 ha, cattle establishments always point. Geometry kind vs computed area mismatch is a hard finding.
  - **Declared-vs-computed area:** the supplier's own declared area compared with the computed area at a stated tolerance; absent declared area is `unstated`, not a pass.
  - **Cross-plot integrity:** pairwise overlap above tolerance → `overlap` (double-count risk); identical vertex sets → `duplicate`. This is the check that a single-file validator cannot make.
  - **Yield plausibility:** declared quantity vs computed plot area × the **operator's own** yield figure (an input, never a vendored assumption) → an over-claim is a finding, not a rejection.
  - **Scope & applicability:** vendored, version-stamped Annex I CN-code table (the 18 September 2026 consolidated list) → per product line: in scope / out / `unverifiable code` (blocks, never guesses); applicability rules applied (30 December 2026 for large and medium operators and timber SMEs, 30 June 2027 for micro and small non-timber operators; codes added by the 2026 act from 30 December 2027).
  - **Completeness invariant:** `Σ(rows parsed) == Σ(rows in input)` — a silently dropped supplier row is itself a finding.
  - **Fix-request pack:** per supplier and per plot, the exact element to resend, in plain language, ready to forward.
- **Inputs / Outputs:** a supplier submission set (mixed formats, model-extracted) → structured JSON (per-plot findings, portfolio invariants, scope screen) + a downloadable pre-flight pack and supplier fix-request list.
- **Test vectors:** a clean set that must produce zero findings; a >4 ha plot sent as a point (hard finding); two suppliers' overlapping polygons (double-count finding); declared tonnage above area × yield (finding); an unreadable PDF region (blocks); a CN code whose status the 2026 act changed (flags, never guess).
- **Out of the 4-hour cap (recorded, not faked):** country-boundary containment and satellite/forest-cover screening. Containment needs a vendored boundary dataset the factory cannot maintain honestly; the deforestation-risk raster check is owned by the paid platforms and needs imagery. Neither is proposed.
- **Rule-5 / extraction posture:** all geometry and arithmetic runs locally in the browser for the free preview; the model is a field-reader, never an arithmetician.
