### 1. The Bottleneck & Market Context

- **Current State:** a payer pays *less* than the contracted rate, the claim closes as paid, and the shortfall is invisible in every denial report the practice runs. Detection requires comparing the allowed amount on every 835 service line against the payer's contracted rate table — line by line, payer by payer, every remittance. The rate table usually lives in contract PDFs and fee-schedule exhibits, and the recovery, once found, is an appeal chase.
- **Measured magnitude (sources dated in §7):** 1–3% of net patient revenue lost annually, 5–7% where contract management is weak, MGMA 7–11% including underpaid/unpaid claims; 3–11% of insurance payments reported to contain underpayment errors; a small practice (3–5 providers, $3M–$5M collections) carries **$30,000–$150,000/yr** at risk. One radiology group validated **$1.1M from a single payer**; Blue Cross Blue Shield's underpayment settlement was **$2.8B** and an arbitration panel ordered UnitedHealthcare to pay Envision **$91.2M**.
- **Named causes (all arithmetic, not judgement):** rate applied from a stale fee schedule after renegotiation; annual escalators never applied; carve-out procedures paid at the standard rate; payer bundling lines that are separately payable; downcoding without clinical justification; modifiers ignored; duplicate adjustment reason codes; payer internal policy applied against the contract.
- **Validation Intensity:** the same failure mode is described independently by at least eight 2026 industry sources, three of them vendors of the fix — the tell that the pain is real and monetisable. This is a *loud* signal; it failed on defence, not on demand.

### 2. Architecture & Technical Blueprint (what would have been built)

- **Engine Type:** parser (archetype **b**) + audit engine (archetype **a**) + appeal-packet generator (archetype **d**).
- **Deterministic core (`src/lib/calc/payervariance/`):** X12 835 parser (ISA/GS/ST envelopes with control-number and segment-count validation; BPR/TRN; CLP/CAS/SVC/NM1/PLB; CARC/RARC lookup), the rate resolver (contracted rate table from pasted/CSV rows only — no PDF/OCR), the variance engine (`allowed` vs contract rate per code/modifier, with group-code classification separating true variance from contractual write-off and patient responsibility), the balancing invariants (`Σ(SVC payments) + Σ(CAS) == CLP paid`; `Σ(claim payments) == BPR amount`; PLB attribution), and the appeal-packet emitter for the line items whose dispute window is still open.
- **Rule-5 posture:** a code with no contracted rate is reported `unverifiable-rate` and **never** defaulted from a benchmark; an unstated contract term blocks a pass verdict. All parsing client-side — no PHI leaves the browser, which is also the only honest way a small vendor can ship this without a business-associate agreement.
- **Test vectors:** a remittance where a 90837 was paid at the 90834 rate; a batch whose allowed amount drifted 4% on re-processing; a `Σallocations == invoice.total`-style balancing break; a clean file that must produce **zero** findings.

### 3. Why it is NOT proposed (the three gates it fails)

1. **Free incumbent (Stage 2 drop rule).** Free tooling already covers the self-serve layer:
   - the **AMA's free Claims Workflow Assistant** — "helps your practice understand... electronic remittance advice (ERA) transactions and address any issues with how the insurer handled your claims", recently updated to the latest code combinations, with free member appeal-letter templates (https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/digital-health/insurers-not-paying-correctly-free-tool-helps-address-denials; https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/claims-processing/tools-proper-payment-appeals);
   - free 835 parsers/viewers: Healthcare Data Insight's free EDI viewer (limits 25 KB / 3 claims), eMedcoders' free in-browser 835 parser, Stedi's free validator tier, open-source `pyx12` and the `era_835_parser` gem;
   - free deadline calculators (timely filing/appeal windows) and free appeal-letter generators;
   - **PM/EHR-side contract-variance reporting** — athenahealth's own guidance teaches practices that stale fee schedules cause underpayments, and third-party Athena reporting packs include contract-variance reports. The practice that can act already has a report.
   The paid tier is real but **sales-led and priced away from this buyer**: payer-contract management platforms publish prices "starting at $500/month per team", healthcare CLMs sit at "tens of thousands annually", and enterprise CLMs "start at $30K–$200K+ first-year" (MD Clarity comparison pages, 2026). A practice that would not buy a $500/mo platform is served free by the AMA and its PM system.
2. **Filter 1 — the recoverable money is a human service.** Every source that describes the fix describes people doing it: "seasoned underpayment recovery specialists who pursue every flagged variance through resolution" (MD Clarity), Revecore's underpayment-recovery practice, Advantum, StafGo, Sirius, MBW. Detection without the chase is exactly the free layer; the paid layer is rule-1-forbidden.
3. **Stage 0 channel rule — no owned audience.** `healthcare_rcm` is not one of the 26 editorial verticals; there is no owned persona, source list or publishing surface for a medical-billing buyer, only community posting (billing forums/Reddit), and the person in those communities (a biller) is usually not the person who signs. Per the ledger rule, no build slot without a channel.

**Honest weakness found in the evidence itself:** the pain is evergreen. The magnitude sources are June–August 2026; the genuinely in-window (last 30 days) items are reminders and supplier content, not a dated trigger for this buyer. The nearest dated driver — the CY2027 Medicare fee-schedule cut — is still a **proposed** rule (issued 2026-07-14, comments closed 2026-09-14, final expected in the fall, effective 2027-01-01: conversion factor proposed −1.19% to $33.17 / −1.69% to $32.84 as the 2.5% one-year bump expires). "Urgency" here is a January renewal cycle, not a deadline in the scan window.
